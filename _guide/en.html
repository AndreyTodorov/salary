The short version

Your net pay is your gross pay minus four kinds of deductions, all of which your employer withholds and pays on your behalf:

  • Payroll tax (Lohnsteuer), the monthly prepayment of your income tax.
  • Solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag, “Soli”), which only higher earners still pay.
  • Church tax (Kirchensteuer), only if you belong to a church that levies it.
  • Social contributions for health, long-term care, pension and unemployment insurance.

Up to the contribution caps, social contributions take a fixed share of about 21 % of your gross pay. Payroll tax is progressive: the more you earn, the larger the share it takes.

Social contributions in 2026

You and your employer each pay about half of every contribution. Pay above the monthly cap is contribution-free, which is why net pay rises faster once your salary passes €5,812.50 and again at €8,450.

  • Private health insurance: you pay your insurer’s premium yourself, and your employer pays half of it as a tax-free subsidy, up to €508.59 for health and €104.63 for care insurance a month in 2026.

How payroll tax is calculated

Your employer projects your monthly pay to a full year, subtracts the allowances, applies the income tax tariff and divides the result by twelve. The allowances built into payroll tax are:

  • the employee allowance (Arbeitnehmer-Pauschbetrag) of €1,230 for work expenses,
  • a €36 allowance for special expenses (Sonderausgaben-Pauschbetrag),
  • a lump sum for your pension, health, care and unemployment contributions (Vorsorgepauschale),
  • in tax class 2, the single-parent allowance of €4,260.

For married couples, the tax class only changes how much is withheld each month. The actual tax is settled in the joint tax return, so 3/5 and 4/4 lead to the same tax for the year.

Solidarity surcharge and church tax

The Soli is 5.5 % of your payroll tax, but only once your yearly payroll tax exceeds €20,350 (€40,700 in class 3). Above that threshold it is phased in gradually. In class 1, that means no Soli up to a gross salary of roughly €7,700 a month, and the full 5.5 % from about €11,200.

Church tax is 9 % of your payroll tax, or 8 % in Baden-Württemberg and Bavaria. You only pay it as a member of a church that levies it.

Children do not reduce payroll tax itself, because parents receive child benefit (Kindergeld) instead. They do reduce Soli and church tax, which are calculated as if the child allowance of €9,756 per child had been deducted. In classes 1 and 2 each child usually counts half, because parents share the allowance.

13th salary, holiday pay and other one-off payments

Bonuses such as a 13th salary or holiday pay (Urlaubsgeld) are taxed as one-off payments (sonstige Bezüge). Your employer calculates the tax on your yearly pay with the bonus and without it, and withholds the difference. The bonus is therefore taxed at your highest marginal rate, so less of it is left than of a normal monthly salary.

The Soli works differently on a bonus: if your yearly tax including the bonus passes the threshold, the full 5.5 % is withheld on the tax on the bonus, with no phase-in. Your tax return settles the exact amount.

Social contributions on a bonus are only due up to the room left under the contribution cap for the year so far (§ 23a SGB IV). If your monthly salary is already above a cap, the bonus is free of that contribution.

Benefit cards (Sachbezug)

Many employers give a prepaid benefit card. Benefits in kind are tax- and contribution-free up to €50 a month. This is an exemption limit, not an allowance: at €50.01 the whole amount becomes taxable. Some employers pay the tax and contributions on a larger benefit themselves, in which case your net pay is not affected. Your payslip shows which applies.

Why your payslip can differ

  • Your health insurer’s Zusatzbeitrag is above or below the 2.9 % average.
  • You have an allowance registered with the tax office (Freibetrag), or you and your spouse use the factor method.
  • Your pay includes a company pension (bAV) by salary conversion, capital-forming benefits (VWL), a company car or other benefits.
  • You earn €2,000 a month or less, where special midijob rules apply.
  • Deductions after net pay, such as the canteen, reduce the amount paid out but not your net pay.

Payroll tax is a prepayment. Your actual income tax is settled in your tax return, where work expenses above €1,230, for example, can lead to a refund.
